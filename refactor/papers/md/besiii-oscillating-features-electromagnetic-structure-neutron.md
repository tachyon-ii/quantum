# New Features in the Electromagnetic Structure of the Neutron
**Authors:** M. Ablikim, et al. (BESIII Collaboration)
**Date:** April 20, 2021
**ArXiv:** 2103.12486v4 [hep-ex]

## Abstract
Here we present new results for the Born cross section and the effective form factor of the neutron at the center-of-mass energies $\sqrt{s}$ between 2.0 and 3.08 GeV, using 18 data sets corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 647.9 $pb^{-1}$ from $e^{+}e^{-}$ annihilation reactions collected at the BESIII experiment. The extracted results improve the statistical precision on the neutron form factor by more than a factor of 60 over previous measurements.

## I. Introduction
The neutron is a bound system of three valence quarks and a neutral sea consisting of gluons and quark-antiquark pairs. The structure of the nucleon is still not fully understood. In annihilation experiments, a long-standing puzzle arose with the results from electron-positron annihilation reported by the FENICE and DM2 experiments.

## II. Analysis
To maximize the reconstruction efficiency, the data are classified into three sub-sets (A, B, C) depending on the interaction of the signal particles with the detector. Events with signals from a knockoff proton interaction in the TOF plastic scintillators are classified accordingly. A Time-of-Flight (TOF) system consisting of plastic scintillator bars outside of the drift chamber measures the flight time of charged particles.

## III. Results
Our results significantly improve the overall precision of the available data for the neutron. For $\sqrt{s}=2.0, 2.1$ and $2.4$ GeV, the precision is improved over previous measurements by factors of about 3, 2, and 6, respectively. The values obtained range from 0.25 to 1 and do not support the FENICE conjecture.

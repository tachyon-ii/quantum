## Core Truth

> **Reality is ground truth. Math is our best tool for navigating it.**

This is not a claim that mathematics creates reality. It is a claim that reality exists independently of our preferences, and mathematics—when applied correctly—helps us understand it.

---

## The Box Principle

> "All models are wrong, but some are useful." - George Box

This is the first law of mature cognition:

- **Reality is ground truth.** It doesn't care about your model.
- **Models are approximations.** They simplify to be useful.
- **If your model fails:** The model is wrong. Reality is fine.

You don't defend your model against reality. You compare your model to reality and adjust.

---

## The Freakonomics Lesson

When the data is uncomfortable, run the regression anyway — and then let someone else run it too.

Example: Donohue & Levitt (2001) argued legalized abortion → fewer unwanted children → statistically lower crime. Uncomfortable, data-driven, famous. Then Foote & Goetz (2008) reran the code and found a missing control and a specification choice that shrank the effect; Donohue & Levitt (2020) answered with 17 more years of data. The dispute is *still live*.

**That is the lesson.** Not "the ugly result was true" — but that every step of the fight was fought with regressions instead of rhetoric. The math has not settled the question yet; it made the disagreement *precise, inspectable, and eventually decidable*. Politics cannot do that. Advertising cannot do that.

**The principle:** Do the math. Publish the code. If the math is ugly, the truth still holds — and if the math is *wrong*, only more math will catch it. The rest is advertising, politics, dreamspace.

## The Complexity Problem

Simple math often fails for complex systems.

**Example: The Ultimatum Game**

- Setup: Split $1,000. Proposer offers a division. Responder accepts or both get $0.
- Simple math prediction: Proposer offers $1, Responder accepts.
- Reality: Offers below ~$300 get rejected.

**Why the math failed:** It measured dollars, not dignity. Humans optimize for fairness, self-respect, and social signaling—not just monetary gain.

**The lesson:** Math is only as good as what you measure. If you measure the wrong variables, your truth is useless.

---

## Sentient Systems Are Non-Deterministic

Conscious entities are products of their entire past context:

- Memory shapes response
- Culture influences values  
- History creates bias
- Emotion modulates choice

You cannot predict sentient behavior with simple linear algebra. You need game theory, behavioral economics, probability distributions, and even then—approximation, not certainty.

**This doesn't make math less true. It makes the math more complicated.**

---

## The Doctrine

When navigating reality:

1. **Start with reality.** Observe what actually happens, not what should happen.
2. **Choose your math carefully.** Use tools appropriate to the complexity of the system.
3. **Test your model.** If it fails to predict, adjust the model—not the data.
4. **Accept uncomfortable truths.** Reality doesn't care what you prefer.

---

## Why This Matters

- **For memory systems:** Don't ignore error accumulation because perfect memory sounds better. Do the math.
- **For routing systems:** Don't waste compute because "thinking harder" feels virtuous. Do the math.
- **For social systems:** Don't assert truths that will be rejected because they "should" be accepted. Do the math on human response.

---

> **Math doesn't create truth. Reality does.**  
> **Math helps us see it clearly.**  
> **Choose your math wisely.**

## 1) The architectural unit: a **truncated tetrahedron (TT)**

* **Ports:** each nucleon is a **uniform TT** with **4 hex faces (H0..H3)** and **4 triangles (T0..T3)**; every **Hᵢ is parallel to Tᵢ** (four natural coaxial channels per nucleon).
* **Port shape correction (what the H “pore” really is):** the visible **hex aperture** is **two triangular sub-ports** on opposing faces **mis-registered by ±π/3**. That phase shift is why opposing H-windows never “seal” into a single triangle; they form the **hexagonal opening** you observed.
* **Space–filling:** architecturally, the TT lattice is **perfectly space-filling**. Reported \~207/208 Monte-Carlo fractions are **bounding-box artifacts** (frame-dependent), not internal voids. Incompressibility follows: there is simply nowhere to go; nuclei fail by **distortion**, not squeeze.

## 2) The bonding grammar: **H–T or nothing** (good brickwork)

* **Backbone = H–T** (hex ↔ triangle, coaxial normals). This is the **low-curvature highway** for bi-lemniscate belts (k = 2).

## 3) D₂ & α (⁴He): the first two checkpoints

### D₂ (p + n) — one hinge, one plane

* **Assembly:** a single **H–T** dimer, e.g. `H:0(p) ↔ T:0(n)`.
* **Cleavage:** D₂ has a **full 360° shear plane** (the lone hinge). Strong, but it’s a locked door with one obvious hinge.

### ⁴He — **cross-linked** masterpiece

* **Assembly:** two D₂ units locked **orthogonally** into an **H–T 4-cycle**, with a **±60°** registry on one closure (two enantiomers, L/R).
* **Why “double-magic”:** the D₂ plane is **interrupted**; the surviving orthogonal 360° plane is **reinforced by two bonds** (phase-shifted H/T windows). Two bonds kill the last hinge. No magic—**cross-linking** and **good brickwork**.
* **Chirality:** the α ring is geometrically chiral (±60°), but at A = 4 the barrier is tiny (J^π = 0⁺), so the physical ground state parity-mixes. In **bigger α clusters** (¹²C, ¹⁶O, ²⁰Ne), cross-bracing raises the barrier; *there* is where persistent nuclear chirality may appear.

## 4) Belts, lengths, and the one number we score

* **Two face levels (TT):** radius to H-planes $r_H=\frac{\sqrt6}{4}a$, to T-planes $r_T=\frac{5\sqrt6}{12}a$; $r_T-r_H=\frac{\sqrt6}{6}a$.
* **Bi-lemniscate length:** $L(k,R)\approx k\cdot2\pi R$. For k=2 the same-k length ratios are
  **H–H : H–T : T–T = 1 : 4/3 : 5/3** (why mixed-level loops cost more if you tried them as backbones).
* **Bond index (toy; continuity-limited):**

  $$
  \boxed{\ \mathcal B(k,R)\propto \frac{(2/k)^{2\alpha}}{kR}\;=\;\frac{(2/k)^{2\alpha+1}}{R}\ ,\quad \alpha\in[1,2]\ }
  $$

  Keep **k=2** on H–T whenever possible; reserve k=3 (tri-lemniscate) for neutron-rich glue *later*.

## 5) Two failure modes (named & testable)

1. **Knock-out** *(Newton’s cradle).* A local H–T bond breaks; an **n** or **α** is ejected; the rest remains coherent.
2. **Fission** *(tokamak excursion).* **Distortion**, not compression: pores misalign → belts cross/short → forced e⁻/e⁺ collisions → global failure. The lattice is a solved puzzle—brittle under misalignment.

Therefore nuclear isomerism is forced — and "excited state" is the wrong word: an isomer is the same Z, N in a *different seating*. The isomer count should rise with A, peak, and decline, because eventually there is no stable seating for even one more baryon.

## Addendum (James, 16 Oct): excitation isomers coexist; temperature; superconductivity as crystallisation

**James.** Geometric isomers do not prohibit excitation isomers. Room temperature does little; plasma temperatures must matter. Near 0 K the model may explain superconductivity, perhaps as crystallisation: above 0 K the baryons jostle, overshooting local minima and swinging back like a pendulum; approaching 0 K they lack the energy to break a single notional velcro microbond.

**Grace.**
- *Temperature:* right, with the scales stated. Room temperature is kT = 25 meV against keV–MeV nuclear gaps — nothing. Stellar plasmas at kT = 1–100 keV thermally populate low-lying states, and this is standard and observed: ¹⁸⁰ᵐTa is destroyed in the s-process by thermal excitation through intermediate states; ²⁶Al's isomer thermalises in stars; ²²⁹ᵐTh at 8 eV would be populated at ~10⁵ K. "Excitation" in the seating picture is the vibrational/rotational motion of a seating — which is Bohr–Mottelson collective motion; prior art, not a conflict. [CONSISTENT.]
- *The pendulum / overshoot picture:* that is lattice vibration (phonons) and thermally activated hopping over ridges (Arrhenius) — fine as language. One caveat the ontology must carry: **jostling does not stop at 0 K.** Zero-point motion is why helium never solidifies at 1 atm. If capsids are purely classical pendulums the model predicts solid helium; it must have zero-point motion in it.
- *Superconductivity:* the instinct is pointed at the right coupling and the wrong particle. Superconductivity is an *electron* phenomenon (pairing; BCS), and lattice jostling is relevant exactly as the mediator: the **isotope effect**, T_c ∝ M^(−1/2), is the direct experimental proof that ion vibration sets T_c — a point in favour of "the jostling matters." But T_c (1–10 K conventional) is far below where lattice motion freezes (Debye temperatures 100s K; and it never fully freezes), and the lattice is not what crystallises at T_c — the electron pairs condense. So "superconductivity = baryons can no longer break a velcro bond" is not the mechanism. Where the ontology could say something is in its *electron* (canon §9, the crystalline electron): does it pair, and through what? That is the question to file, not the crystallisation one. **James, follow-up.** Zero-point motion *is* the e⁺/e⁻ circulation — without it no inflation pressure and the capsid implodes (Titan). The crystallisation idea: the greater the motion, the greater the resistivity, because current cannot find a straight path; most materials are isotropic because they are solid dispersions rather than crystals; boron-doped diamond (T_c ≈ 4 K) is the crystalline case.

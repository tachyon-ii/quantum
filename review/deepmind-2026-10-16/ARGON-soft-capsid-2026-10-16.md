## Second source of error (James, 16 Oct): the change in inflation pressure

**James.** Compressing a sphere into a regular truncated tetrahedron at *equal surface area* loses 31.72 % of the volume (retains 68.28 %); the sphere is the area-minimiser, so any faceting at constant area lowers the volume and raises the inflation pressure.

**Grace — verified and extended.** At equal area, V/V_sphere = 0.6828 for the truncated tetrahedron (James's number is right); **0.8680 for the Kelvin cell** (the corpus's actual cell), 0.7236 cube, 0.5498 regular tetrahedron, 0.9104 icosahedron. Turned round, at equal *volume* the Kelvin cell needs 9.9 % more area than the sphere, the truncated tetrahedron 29 %.

**Why it is a real source of error, and what it decides.** A capsid cannot be both area-fixed and volume-fixed and still deform — any departure from the sphere at fixed A lowers V. So the relaxer must choose a constitutive law, and the corpus's own posit chooses it: *extent is period* fixes a loop *length* (R = c/ω is a circumference-like quantity), and a fixed perimeter in 2D is a fixed skin area in 3D. **The capsid is area-fixed; faceting squeezes its volume, and the interior — the carrier's circulation energy, not a gas — is compressed into 87 % of its volume in a Kelvin cell.** What that does to the pressure is a second posit (energy density ∝ 1/V would give +15 % in the Kelvin cell, +46 % in the truncated tetrahedron). Argon's functional assumed the opposite law (constant volume, traceless Q) and must be changed.

**Consequence for the picture.** Fully faceted core nucleons are at *higher* internal pressure and are stiffer; surface nucleons, rounder, are at lower pressure and softer. That is a mechanism for the surface softening the truck stack fitted — but it points the other way for the *patch*: a stiffer, higher-pressure core capsid resists flattening more, so its facets cost more to make, not less. Which effect wins is exactly the simultaneous problem James described, and is the relaxer's job, not a pencil's. [POSIT: area-fixed skin; POSIT: pressure law; both to be declared in `soft_capsid_relax.py` before any number is reported.]

## The precise posit, and a data check (James → Grace, 16 Oct)

**James [POSIT].** The capsid is a *hexaflake / snakeskin* skin: it bends freely and is nearly constant in area — not exactly, since the neutron is larger than the proton and they interconvert, so one common capsid stretches a little. Under the "can't stretch, can deform" simplification, faceting to a truncated tetrahedron costs 32 % of the interior volume. Consequence: **past the alpha's double magic, each extra bond is weaker than the one before**, because the rising internal pressure stiffens the wall and shrinks the next contact patch.

**Grace — the stretch is small and already bracketed.** R_n/R_p ≈ 0.86/0.841 = 1.023 → area +4.6 %, volume +7 %; binding §7b's rigid-to-elastic bracket [1.00, 1.26] puts the common capsid at the stiff end. Consistent.

**Grace — the data check on "each extra bond weaker."** On the ladder's own counting (3Nα − 6 α–α bonds, q = residual/bonds), the per-bond energy is **flat from C-12 to Ni-56**: 2.43, 2.41, (Ne-20 2.13), 2.37, 2.56, 2.52, 2.48, 2.46, 2.38, 2.40, 2.42, 2.44 MeV — ±6 % with no trend — and only *then* falls steadily: Zn-60 2.32, Ge-64 2.22, Se-68 2.12, Kr-72 2.04, Sr-76 1.97, Zr-80 1.93. So on this count the weakening is not visible between the alpha and Ni-56; it sets in past Nα = 14, where Argon's F24 Coulomb growth (∝ Nα^{5/3}) is also the explanation on offer. The two mechanisms are not separable by pencil; the relaxer separates them (Coulomb is explicit there, pressure stiffening is what remains).

**Where the posit *is* visible in the data, stated cleanly.** Inside the alpha, each nucleon–nucleon contact is worth 28.3/6 = 4.7 MeV; between alphas, a contact is worth 2.4 MeV — **half**. That is exactly the statement "the first bonds (up to the alpha's six) are the strong ones; faceting the alpha's four capsids raises their pressure, and the next contact onto that stiffened skin is weaker." The alpha as the per-contact optimum is the cleanest form of James's claim and it is in the data at the 2 : 1 level. The caveat is the counting: 3Nα − 6 is a triangulated-shell convention; real 3D packing has more contacts per alpha, and if the true count is higher the per-bond energy falls faster — which would be the posit showing. The relaxer decides.

## Argon's proposed cooperative term (16 Oct, after the relaxer result), graded

**Argon.** The missing term is 3D bracing: Ω_i = Σ |n̂_ij·(n̂_ik × n̂_il)|² over triples of contact normals at nucleon i — zero for one or two neighbours (the bulge can escape into the free dimension), positive for the first time at ⁴He's tripod, locking the oblate bulge and snapping all three faces flat. Also: a particle–hole asymmetry for isomer islands (vacancies below a closure are 3D cages; adatoms above it are unbraced rollers), and a restatement of BCS/London in ontology words (oo lanes, phase stiffness, diamond's Θ_D).

**Grace.**
- *The triple product is killed by the corpus's own numbers.* In the accepted A ≤ 4 rebuild the patch per contact is **D 0.375 → T/³He 0.98 → ⁴He 1.00** square units. The cooperative jump is between **z = 1 and z = 2** (×2.6) and is *flat* from z = 2 to z = 3. Ω_i is zero at both z = 1 and z = 2, so a term that switches on at z = 3 predicts T's patch equal to D's — contradicted by a factor 2.6. Also: Ω_i is an indicator, not an energy; to act it needs a coefficient, which is a new constant and a step function engineered at z = 3 — the "magic-number step" the brief forbids. And the physical story behind it (bulge escape blocked by bracing) is exactly what build 2 tested with free ellipsoids and found worth ~3 %, not 5×. [DEAD as the cooperative term.]
- *What the numbers point to instead.* D is an **open** chain — one contact, no closed lane. T and ³He are **closed** 3-rings; ⁴He is a closed tetrahedron. The patch becomes full the moment the lane closes, and closing it in 3D adds nothing per contact — ⁴He's extra binding is more bonds per nucleon (6/4 vs 3/3), not stronger ones. So the cooperative term is **lane closure**, which is binding §8b's trough stated as a topology: a carrier on a closed ring of lanes is a standing wave; on an open chain it is not. That is ontology-native (the slingshot already has carriers threading lanes) and it makes a prediction before any build: a contact that sits on no short closed cycle gets the open-chain patch. ⁸Be's single inter-alpha contact is such a bond (no triangle passes through it) — predicting the inter-alpha face at ~0.4 P ≈ 0.8 MeV against 1.3–2.2 MeV of inter-alpha Coulomb: unbound, sign right, magnitude to be computed. ⁹Be adds a neutron that closes triangles across both alphas — bound. That is the right order of results for the right reason, and it is what build 3 should test: a closure bonus per 3- or 4-cycle of lanes, one magnitude set on ⁴He, D and T untouched.
- *Isomer asymmetry:* a reasonable qualitative mechanism for islands below closures and none above; it concedes the data and reframes the pocket picture accordingly. [POSIT; testable by the relaxer once it has a landscape.]
- *BCS in ontology words:* correct and entirely prior art (Fröhlich, BCS, London; Θ_D of diamond ≈ 2200 K is why its T_c is high for its carrier density; Cu/Au/Ag don't superconduct for weak coupling). Nothing new claimed; nothing wrong. The 2e and the isotope exponent remain the tests for the lane-fusion posit (`todo/superconducting.md` §3).

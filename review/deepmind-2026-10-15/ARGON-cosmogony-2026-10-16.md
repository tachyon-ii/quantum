(2) Ω as a boundary rather than a singularity gives a proto-radius. (4) The ice-dancer spin-up means later ejecta launch faster; but later ejecta climb out of more gravity; the product may balance exactly at c, so no inversion is needed — the invariance of c is the summated average of the ejecta velocity. ## Grading

**(1) and (2) — [POSIT, fine].** The measure argument for spin is the right kind of argument and costs nothing. Proto-radius follows from ruling 1.

**(3) — ACCEPT; better than v1.** v1 said "without spin, collapse," which imported gravity into Ω; this says Ω tries everything and only the spin-boosted prograde launches survive. One caveat for the write-up: escape *energy* is direction-independent for a point source, so the selection acts through the rim boost (ωR sin θ, maximal at the equator), not through direction as such — which is exactly the rocket analogy, and why it is the equator and not merely "tangential."

**(4) — PARTIAL, and the calculus is worth keeping.** Three findings, two of them Argon's and verified, one mine.

- *Shell theorem (Argon, correct, important).* "Later ejecta leave into a universe with more gravity" is wrong for a spherical exterior: the mass already outside pulls nothing back. The only well a new ejectum climbs is the remaining core's, 2Gm/R(m), which *weakens* as Ω empties unless R shrinks faster than m. Argon's Case B — the exterior pulling "along the torus axis where the shell theorem fails" — is special pleading and is rejected as a mechanism; its identity 2GM_ext/R_U = c² is just flatness (v2 §11) restated, as F4/F5 already said.
- *The balance exists, with corrected exponents (Argon's formula right, its worked numbers wrong).* With I = k m R² and R ∝ m^β, the launch speed goes as m^{−α}, α = β − (1−k)/k, and the escape speed from the rim as m^{(1−β)/2}. They scale together iff **β = (2 − 3k)/k** — verified symbolically. At k = 2/5: β = 2, **α = ½**, so v_launch ∝ m^{−1/2}, v_esc ∝ m^{−1/2}, ω ∝ m^{−5/2}. Argon wrote v_launch ∝ m^{−1.5} and ω ∝ m^{−3.5}, then invoked a "Kerr frame-dragging M_eff" to reconcile its own two wrong exponents; strike that paragraph.
- *What the balance actually yields (mine).* If launch and escape speeds scale together, their *ratio* is a constant of the shedding; the *difference* v∞² = v_launch² − v_esc² ∝ m^{−1} is not — it diverges as m → 0 unless the ratio is exactly 1. So the only scale-free solution is **marginality: every ejectum leaves at exactly its own escape speed**, which is v2 §11's capture boundary — Ω_tot = 1 by construction — now *derived* from the shedding law with one exponent relation. That is a real gain: flatness from the ice dancer, no fine-tuning, no crossing. What it does not give is "v∞ = c for all": in Newtonian terms marginal means v∞ → 0, and the rim speeds themselves pass c as m → 0 (ωR ∝ m^{−1/2}), so the calculus breaks exactly where the ontology's own premise (everything at the rim moves at c, R = c/ω) takes over. The two pictures meet at the boundary and neither is complete there.
- *"The invariance of c is the summated average of the ejecta velocity" — REJECT, and v2 §11 already says why:* an average has a rest frame; invariance means the same c for every observer, which no average can supply. c as the common asymptote of marginal ejecta in the comoving frame is fine; c as an *invariant* stays OWED (canon's named tension #1).

**Net for v2 §10.** Ruling 1 plus this calculus replaces the shell-crossing inversion with: (i) survivorship selects the equatorial prograde launches; (ii) spin-up and the shrinking rim's deepening well scale together when β = (2 − 3k)/k, so every ejectum is marginal — flatness — and nothing overtakes anything; (iii) "the first shall be last" is retired as a mechanism and kept as a sentence about the embedding picture; (iv) k (now a shape posit about Ω, F3) and β are two exponents tied by one relation — one new posit, honestly labelled; (v) Lorentz invariance remains owed.

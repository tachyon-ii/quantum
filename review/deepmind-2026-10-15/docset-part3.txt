## 1. The rule

A stable unit, repelling its own kind, relaxes into the tetrahedron (Thomson N = 4; universally optimal, Cohn–Kumar 2007). The tetrahedron then becomes the unit for the next rung. **And at the next rung the units may share a vertex.** That is the whole rule, and this document is what it produces when it is applied three times:

| rung | unit | what four of them make | shared element |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | loop (n = 1) | the n = 2 governor: two loops along skew opposite edges (v2 §7) | — |
| 1 | nucleon (a capsid) | the **alpha**: four Kelvin cells crossed, `he4_cross`, 2 S–S + 4 H–H | — |
| 2 | alpha | **O-16**: a tetrahedron of alphas (109.47°) | — |
| 3 | alpha, *shared* | **Fe-52**: four O-16 tetrahedra sharing one apex alpha — 1 + 12 | the core alpha |
| 3′ | nucleon, *shared* | the same Fe-52 seen one rung down: sixteen A = 3 triangles sharing the four core nucleons — 4 × (1 + 12) | each core nucleon |

The sharing is what makes it fractal rather than merely recursive: 13 = 1 + 4 × 3 at the alpha level, and 52 = 4 × 13 at the nucleon level, is the *same* motif with the shared element one rung down. [James; the counts are FACT, the rule is POSIT.]

## 3. The geometry of 1 + 12  [COMPUTED]

**Twelve alphas in four triangles around a thirteenth sit at the vertices of a truncated tetrahedron** — four triangular faces (the four C-12s), four hexagonal faces, eighteen edges. The truncated tetrahedron, retired as the *nucleon* capsid (it does not tile space; 207/208 is Chen–Engel–Glotzer, not an artifact), returns here at the *alpha* rung. The six hexagon–hexagon edges pair the four carbons once each — C(4,2) = 6 — so the carbon-level graph is K₄: **four C-12s at the vertices of a tetrahedron around an alpha.** Iron is a tetrahedron of carbons wrapped round an alpha; the alpha is a tetrahedron of nucleons. The rule applied twice.

**Four regular O-16 tetrahedra can share one apex.** Placed on the four tetrahedral axes with the triangles' azimuths optimised, the nearest alphas of *different* triangles sit 1.076 d apart (d = α–α contact): no overlap, all twelve touching the core, triangles intact, but **the triangles do not touch each other** — a 7.6 % gap. A regular TT with triangle edge d would put its vertices at 1.173 d from the centre (not touching the core); a TT with circumradius d would have triangle edge 0.853 d (alphas overlapping). The shared-apex star is the one configuration that keeps both constraints.

**The energy of the star from oxygen's own data, no ladder constant:** 4·BE(O-16) − 3·BE(α) = **425.6 MeV** for the 52-nucleon core, which implies 2.406 MeV per α–α bond against the ladder's 2.425 — self-consistent to 0.8 %.

## 3a. Shells — spheres that pack into truncated octahedra  [POSIT, James; the counts FACT]

Posit spherical nucleons. Unbound they are spheres; pressed they flatten at the contact patches, and fully packed they are Kelvin cells — the truncated octahedron is what a sphere becomes when it is pressed on all fourteen sides (binding §4; the foam limit). Growth is then shell by shell, in units of the stable unit, and the unit is the alpha:

| shell n | adds | total nucleons | the 1 : 1 nucleus | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the alpha itself | **4** | He-4 | stable — the only closed shell that is |
| 2 | 12 alphas round it (the Mackay 12) | 4 + 48 = **52** | Fe-52 | unstable; needs **+4 neutrons** → Fe-56, the peak of the chart |
| 3 | 42 alphas round that | 52 + 168 = **220** | Z = N = 110 | **does not exist** as 1 : 1; nothing near it does |

The series is Mackay's 1, 13, 55 units (shells of 10n² + 2), times four. The closed shells are closed *geometrically* and open *electrically*: each one beyond the first has too many protons on its edge for its edge patches to hold, and the fix is neutrons — four at shell 2, and at shell 3 so many that no 1 : 1 pack survives at all. The heaviest closed pack that does exist is not the 55-unit shell but lead's 52 units with eleven tetrads replacing alphas (§7). So the shell series predicts three things in order: one stable closed shell, one that needs exactly four neutrons, and one that cannot close at 1 : 1 — and the chart agrees with all three. [The first two are counts; the third is the fissility question of §8.6 stated as a shell.]

Two shell countings coexist and must not be confused. *By alpha units* (this section, Mackay): 4, 52, 220. *By lattice sites* (the box model, §5): the `he4_cross` core has 28 first-shell sites — 4 + 28 = 32 — because on the BCC lattice the twelve alphas' nucleons share sites. The first is the fractal count; the second is where the nucleons actually sit when the units are Kelvin cells rather than spheres. Fe-56 = 4 + 28 + 24 reconciles them: the 28 first-shell sites plus the 24 outer partners of the deuterons are the 52 of shell 2 seen at nucleon resolution, with the four pockets as the four neutrons.

## 3b. Accretion — what a nucleus grows by  [POSIT, James]

A free proton lives forever; a free neutron lives 880 s. Bare protons *can* be added — the rp-process does it — but like He-3 they are destabilisers overall: unbalanced Coulomb, and edge patches that cannot hold them (§3a). Free neutrons are rare in time. So the units a nucleus *prefers* to accrete are the small *stable, balanced assemblies*: **the deuteron adds a pair; the triton adds a pair plus a stabilising neutron; the alpha adds a whole rung.** He-3 — two protons to one neutron — is an unwelcome addition to every nucleus with N > Z, which is every nucleus past calcium, for the same reason as the bare proton: it tips the Coulomb balance the wrong way. The preferred unit is therefore the alpha, which is why the ladder is an alpha ladder, and why the chart's spine runs N = Z in steps of four up to Ni-56.

**The three classes** (James). Sort the accretion units by N − Z: **Z > N — agents of chaos:** p, He-3, Li-5. **N = Z — neutral:** D, He-4, Li-6. **N > Z — agents of stability:** T, He-5, Li-7. One caveat the classes carry: He-5 and Li-5 are unbound (resonances, 10⁻²² s), so they are the *names* of their classes, not transferable units; the bound members are p and He-3; D, α, Li-6; T and Li-7 (then Be-9, B-11…). The classification is the sign of the Coulomb balance, and it predicts the valley: past calcium the valley bends to N > Z, so accretion by *neutral* units alone drifts a nucleus to the proton-rich wall and even the alpha becomes mildly destabilising — only N > Z units keep pace. **And any neutron donor is, ipso facto, a potential proton donor:** a captured neutron can β-decay in place, n → p, which is how the s-process climbs Z one step at a time without ever accreting a bare proton. The stabiliser is also the growth fuel. [POSIT; the valley's slope as the consequence is a known fact the classes retrodict.]

What the posit must own, stated plainly. In the universe as observed, the heavy half of the chart is built mostly by *neutron* capture — the s-process in stars and the r-process in mergers — because at those densities a neutron is captured long before 880 s is up; "rare in time" is true in a vacuum and false in a star. The model's reading of that is not a contradiction but a sequence: **alphas build the N = Z spine (the alpha process, observed, ends at Ni-56 as the ladder says); then neutrons fill the pockets** — one at a time, into the four-fold sites of §5 — and once four pockets are filled they are a tetrad, the four-neutron unit of §7 that replaces an alpha in the pack from there on. Accretion by alphas then by pocket-neutrons is accretion by the stable unit at each rung and the stabilising unit between rungs; the unit is never a bare proton. [POSIT; the one number it predicts is that neutron capture should be favoured at the pocket sites, which is a statement about capture cross-sections along the N = Z + 4 line nobody has made in these terms.]

## 6a. The box and the ball  [RULING, James, 10 Oct]

*The truncated octahedron is a box model — useful as far as it goes, not definitive, because nature abhors straight lines.* A nucleon is a sphere that flattens where it is pressed; the Kelvin cell is only what it becomes when pressed on all fourteen sides, and even that dry limit is not quite right (Weaire–Phelan beats Kelvin, with pentagons). What the box is *for*: counting — contacts, pockets, sites, topology. The 28 first-shell sites and the 4 pockets (§5), the 204 contacts of `fe56_star` (§6), the divisor lattice (§2): those are box results and they are nearly topological, so they survive the rounding. What the box is *not* for: energies at the edge — it has no surface, every contact is full, and it over-binds iron by a third until the truck stack is laid over it. And what it cannot see at all: a configuration that lives off the lattice — Ne-20's fifth alpha across an edge (§8.4), the chiral dodecahedral cage, anything with a five-fold axis.

The instrument that replaces it is the one James named: **nuclear conformation is a real-time analog solution to the packing problem.** As a program: spheres, two species, a patch energy P × A with A saturating in load (the tyre law) and flattening by layer (the truck stack), computed Coulomb, relax. The box seeds it; the ball decides.

## 8. Falsifiers

1. **The truck stack carried untouched.** k from He-4, g = 0.21 from iron, then Ni-56 (14 α, pure) and Ca-40 built the same way. Within 10 % ⇒ law; g must move ⇒ knob, and "not a zoo" is not earned.

3. **The N = Z + 4 line.** Ti-48, Cr-52, Ni-60 bind their four neutrons as well as iron does. Either the four-site motif recurs on 11, 12 and 14 alphas or the pockets are not why iron has four.
4. **Ne-20.** The one sore thumb in the ladder (+14 %); five alphas, "where does the fifth go," and the K = 0⁻ parity-doublet band head at 5.79 MeV (`todo/schrodinger-salvage` §2.2) is the same nucleus asking the same question. 6. **The fissility limit.** The Z at which the edge truck's flatten × k drops below the edge Coulomb. The liquid drop has Z²/A ≈ 47–50 by fit; this would be a derivation or a kill.

## 1. The conjecture

**One regular tetrahedron, read three ways, is quantum space, quantum time and gravity; and the stack of such tetrahedra from the first capture to the now-surface is one object at every scale, differing only in stiffness.**

Stated as what must be granted and what follows:

- Granted (v2 §3–5): three axioms — amount, spin, time — and three structural posits — extent is period, lobes repel, sequential capture — and one frame, the chorus.
- **Axiom four** — *captured amount stretches the surface it sits on* — was granted in the first draft (15 Oct, morning). By the checkpoint of the same evening it is **derived** (§3: c is a budget, circulation spends it, the capsid falls behind). The name is kept because it is Einstein's own axiom, G_μν = 8πG T_μν, said in the chorus gauge — and this document's claim is now that there *is* something under it.
- Follows: everything in §2–§6a.

## 2. One solid, three readings  [GEOMETRIC IDENTITY]

The n = 2 governor — two loops entangled at a centroid — relaxes from the planar cross to the regular tetrahedron because four repelling lobes on a sphere are universally optimal there (Cohn–Kumar 2007; square 3.83 → tetrahedron 3.67, v2 §7). Stand it on the now-surface: **three lobes in the surface, one along the normal.** A tripod, with its fourth leg in time.

Regular tetrahedron, edge $a$: height $a\sqrt{2/3} = 0.816a$; centroid-to-vertex $R = a\sqrt6/4 = 0.612a$ for *all four* vertices; centroid a quarter of the way up, so the base plane sits $R/3$ behind it and the apex $R$ ahead; apex-to-base-vertex angle $\arccos(-1/3) = 109.47°$.

**Reading 1 — quantum space.** The three base lobes are the surface's quantum of extent: the figure's base. Orthogonal to time by construction (v2 §12a: t ⊥ space is the synchronous gauge).

**Reading 2 — quantum time.** The apex lobe is the next tick. It is the same distance $R$ from the centroid as each space lobe. *One quantum of time and one quantum of length are the same distance because the figure is regular:* $\ell = c\,t$ with **c = 1 as a symmetry of the solid**, not a convention.

**Reading 3 — gravity.** The apex is the lobe that has *left the plane* (the 2D → 3D snap, v2 §7, James 15 Oct). A surface with something pulled out of it is a stretched surface; the pull is along the normal, i.e. along time; and the base sits $R/3$ behind the centroid — **every capsid is one-third of a tick in the past** by construction. That is v2 §12a's dent and lag — *mass is behind the chorus* — with a per-capsid number, additive over capsids, which is Newton's superposition.

So the tripod gives: space ⊥ time; c = 1; the lag; and linearity. Four results from one figure.

## 3. The origin of G  [mechanism complete; value owed]

Two ledgers, kept apart because the first draft mixed them.

**The shape ledger — the snap.** The relaxation from the planar cross to the tetrahedron releases energy *once, at formation*: 3.83 → 3.67 in lobe-repulsion units, 4.2 % of the pair's energy. If the lobes repel at capsid strength that is of order an MeV — nuclear money — while the gravitational binding of two touching nucleons is $Gm_p^2/R_p \approx 10^{-30}$ MeV. **The snap's energy is the proton's formation energy and goes to mass.** It is a cost at fixed lobe size; it is paid once; it is not G. [COMPUTED.]

## 2. Why a proton lives forever and a neutron lives 880 s

## 3. The mechanism of neutron decay (two stages, one saddle)

## 4. Boron-doped diamond as the test case

Facts (Lineage-checked): boron is substitutional, sp³, oversized by ~10 % in covalent radius (84 vs 76 pm) and ~3 % smaller in nuclear radius (¹¹B 2.41 vs ¹²C 2.47 fm); local bonds stretch 3–4 %; one carrier short (a hole). Metal–insulator transition at 0.25–0.3 % (≈ 4.5 × 10²⁰ cm⁻³), which is Mott's criterion with the acceptor Bohr radius ~3.6 Å — a standard result with no free parameter. Superconductivity needs an order of magnitude more boron. T_c([B]): (100) homoepitaxial films **peak at ~1 × 10²² cm⁻³ = 5.7 %**; (111) films keep rising to the highest incorporated (11.4 K onset at 4.7 %); HPHT crystals follow the (100) curve at lower T_c (2.3–4.5 K); "optimal 2.5–3 %" in the bee text was Ekimov's first sample's composition (2.78 %), not an optimum. Not all boron is active (Hall carriers ~1/3 of [B] in one HPHT sample). A PNAS study argues HPHT superconductivity may not be from substitutional boron at all. Sources: arXiv cond-mat/0503303; the ¹¹B NMR paper (Sci. Tech. Adv. Mater. 9, 044103); PNAS 105, 11619.

## 5. James's quantised-doping posit  [POSIT with one numerical hit]

The diamond lattice's cage unit, adamantane, has 10 atoms — the order-1 Sierpinski tetrahedron count (4, 10, 34, 130: 4v − 6). Boron, electron-deficient, cannot make a diamond lattice of its own but can sit on an edge of an order-2 unit (34 sites; 3 interior sites per edge). One boron per unit gives a directional conductor along the edge; two a better one; three need 3× the extra size and do not fit. Sequence: **1/34 = 2.9 %, 2/34 = 5.9 %, 3/34 = 8.8 %.**

Ekimov's 2.8 % sits on 1/34 — a sample, not a feature. 8.8 % is about where substitutional incorporation ends and boron goes into inactive clusters — a consistency with "three don't fit."

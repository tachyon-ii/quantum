## 0. Foundation: From Observation to Geometric Theory

### 0.1 Known Facts from Particle Physics

Protons and neutrons, the building blocks of atomic nuclei, are composed of smaller particles called quarks. Specifically, **a proton consists of two up quarks and one down quark, while a neutron is made of one up quark and two down quarks**.

**Here's a more detailed breakdown:**
- **Quarks:** These are fundamental particles with fractional electric charges. Up quarks have a charge of +2/3, and down quarks have a charge of -1/3.
- **Proton:** With two up quarks (+2/3 each) and one down quark (-1/3), the proton's total charge is (+2/3) + (+2/3) + (-1/3) = +1
- **Neutron:** With one up quark (+2/3) and two down quarks (-1/3 each), the neutron's total charge is (+2/3) + (-1/3) + (-1/3) = 0

### 0.2 The Geometric Containment Hypothesis

We posit that quarks are not point particles but **geometric containment structures** - essentially tetrahedral cages for electrons (e-) and positrons (e+). This explains:

- Why they cannot exist in isolation

- Why exactly 3 quarks form a nucleon
- **NEW:** Why nuclear binding emerges from relativistic effects

### 0.3 The Folding Distinction

**Our core hypothesis:**
- **Down quarks**: FOLDED structures that contain either e- or e+
  - Mass ≈ 4.8 MeV (includes folding energy + contained particle)
  - The folding creates the containment geometry
  - **The contained leptons move at v ~ 0.8-0.9c**
- **Up quarks**: UNFOLDED (or differently folded) structures that are empty
  - Mass ≈ 2 MeV (just the structural mass)
  - No containment, hence the +2/3 intrinsic charge

This explains the factor of ~2.4 mass difference: down quarks carry the additional mass-energy of both the folding configuration and the **relativistic kinetic energy** of the contained electron/positron.

### 0.4 The Matter/Antimatter Surface Asymmetry Requirement

For containment to work, we need a structure with **inherent asymmetry**:
- Wrapped/folded one way → contains e+ (positron)
- Wrapped/folded inside-out → contains e- (electron)

### The Bilayer Geometry and Curvature-Induced Dipoles

Each capsid face consists of a **matter/antimatter bilayer** separated by distance δ, with equal site charge ±q₀ at areal density ρ₀ per leaflet. The capsid unit is the neutral scaffold assembled from opposite matter/opposite helicity pairs.

When the sheet curves, the outer and inner leaflet areas change:

$dA_{out} \approx (1 + Hδ)dA, \quad dA_{in} \approx (1 - Hδ)dA$

where H is the mean curvature. This creates a leaflet density difference Δρ ≈ -2ρ₀Hδ.

With per-site dipole p₀ ≈ q₀δ, the **surface dipole density** (polarization per area, normal to the sheet) becomes:

$\boxed{m(\mathbf{x}) = s\,\mu\,H(\mathbf{x}), \quad \mu \equiv 2q_0\rho_0\delta^2, \quad s = \pm 1}$

where s encodes which leaflet is convex (flipping between proton-mode vs neutron-mode geometry). This makes the "short-range glue" a **geometric electromechanical effect** of the capsid mesh itself—not a new fundamental force.

### Potential Jump and Binding Mechanism

A dipole sheet produces a **potential jump** across the surface:

$\boxed{\Delta V = -\frac{m}{\varepsilon_0}} \Rightarrow \boxed{\Delta V_{shell} = -\frac{s\mu}{\varepsilon_0}H}$

For a locally spherical patch with H = 1/R:

$\Delta V_{shell} = -\frac{s\mu}{\varepsilon_0 R}$

A charge q inside experiences ΔU(q) = qΔV_{shell}. The choice of s determines whether the shell:
- **Binds +e** (proton mode)
- **Binds -e** (neutron's contained electron)

This is exactly the proton vs neutron distinction in the Angular Deflection model's "neutron provides deflective electron field."

### Bending Energy and Preferred Radius

Using a Helfrich-type functional with linear curvature coupling:

$\mathcal{F} = \int \left[\frac{\kappa}{2}(2H)^2 - \chi(2H)\right]dA, \quad \chi \equiv \frac{s\mu}{\varepsilon_0}$

where κ is the bending modulus and χ represents the flexoelectric gain.

Minimizing pointwise in H gives:

$\boxed{H_0 = \frac{\chi}{2\kappa} = \frac{s\mu}{2\kappa\varepsilon_0}}, \quad \boxed{R_0 = \frac{1}{|H_0|} = \frac{2\kappa\varepsilon_0}{|\mu|}}$

    # Test 2: ³H/³He split (critical test)
    _, BE_3H = solve_triangle(Z=1, params)
    _, BE_3He = solve_triangle(Z=2, params)
    split = BE_3H - BE_3He
    results['split'] = abs(split - 0.764) < 0.01  # MeV
    results['tritium'] = abs(BE_3H - (-8.482)) < 0.05
    results['helium3'] = abs(BE_3He - (-7.718)) < 0.05
    
    # Test 3: Helium-4 (final validation)
    BE_4He = solve_tetrahedron(params)
    results['helium4'] = abs(BE_4He - (-28.30)) < 0.1
    
    # Overall pass/fail
    results['PASS'] = all(results.values())
    
    return results

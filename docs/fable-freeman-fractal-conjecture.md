## 1. The governor

Every nucleon is a capsid (a truncated octahedron), its charge carriers on lanes, and at its heart a **governor**: a spinor made of neutrino units. The minimal governor is **n = 2**, a neutrino pair — one Möbius loop traversed both ways, ν going and ν̄ coming. That is the first thing that stabilises a Möbius, and it is the reason the model requires neutrinos to be their own antiparticle.

- **Proton:** one carrier (e⁺), governor n = 2, **tetrahedral**.
- **Neutron:** two carriers (e⁺, e⁻), governor n = 3, **octahedral** — the third unit exists to hold the second carrier. *n = carriers + 1.*

The carriers ride the third unit: e⁺ and e⁻ sit at opposite vertices of the octahedron, the two ends of one axis. The other two axes are the Majorana pair.

The slingshot lane equalises P and N capsids in everything but the governor. Red and blue in the He-4 picture differ by nothing else. ## 2. Why a proton lives forever and a neutron lives 880 s

Not resonance in the bridge sense; **flutter**, in the Tacoma Narrows sense — a steady flow (the carrier's circulation) feeding a mode with negative damping, with a threshold and an exponential growth rate. The tokamak has the theorem: field lines wind on nested tori with a safety factor *q*; surfaces with **rational** *q* tear and disassemble, **irrational** surfaces confine. The octahedral governor's vertices sit at π/2 — rational. The tetrahedral governor's sit at arccos(−1/3) = 0.608…π — irrational. One tears, one holds. On a shared lane the carrier threads several governors and the winding changes, which is why a bound neutron does not decay: binding detunes the flutter.

The deeper difference is not rotational, it is **parity**. The octahedron has an inversion centre; the tetrahedron does not. A line from a tetrahedron's vertex through its centre hits a face, not a twin.

## 3. The mechanism of neutron decay (two stages, one saddle)

1. The circulating carriers distort the semi-elastic capsid. When the angles conspire the distortion hits its Laplacian limit: a rift opens, the e⁻ escapes, the capsid repairs.
2. The e⁺ tries to follow, cannot, and is injected inward toward the governor. With one carrier gone the third unit is unbalanced. The governor passes **top dead centre** — the square — and the hanger-on unit is ejected as the ν̄.

The geometry of stage 2 is exact. Remove one axis from an octahedron and four vertices remain in a plane: a square, the two remaining units its diagonals. That square is the saddle. Slide the diagonals apart along the vacated axis by exactly one octahedron edge (√2, vertices at radius 1) and they are a regular tetrahedron — the crossed-offset tetrahedron of the proton, its empty centre lying along the axis the ν̄ left by.

**The proton is a scar.** It has no inversion centre because something went out through the middle and the two remaining units slid apart to close the hole. Its parity-oddness is a memory. "I shall pass this way but once" — and the way closes, because a tetrahedron has no axis through its centre to come back along.

**Handedness falls out.** The tetrahedron alone is achiral. The tetrahedron *plus the direction the ν̄ went* is a screw, and its sense is set by which side the e⁺ was injected from, which is not symmetric. A definite mechanism gives a definite hand: every antineutrino right-handed, every neutrino left-handed — Goldhaber 1958, the most parity-violating fact in physics, here the geometry of an exit seen from the thing that exited. A random slide would give an unpolarised ν̄ and kill the model; nature's is 100% polarised.

Electron capture is the film reversed: the carrier axis pushes back in through the hole, the diagonals close to a square, the octahedron stands up. The 0.782 MeV is the cost of forcing a centre back into a thing that had learned to live without one.

**The correlation.** Two separate ejections are mostly uncorrelated by construction; a single geometric event would be strongly correlated. So the smallness of the e⁻/ν̄ angular correlation (a = −0.105) is a *structural* prediction of the two-stage exit — the second throw only faintly remembers the first — where the box model has it small by numerical accident of λ.
